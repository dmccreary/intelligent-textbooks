    Intelligent Textbooks for Electrical Engineering Instructors

    This summary lists the interactive intelligent textbooks from the Case Studies page that are most likely to interest an electrical engineering (EE) instructor. Each entry includes a maturity rating taken from the case studies page, from 1/5 (early development) to 5/5 (complete).

    Source: docs/case-studies/index.md on the main branch (last updated November 7, 2026).

    Core EE Courses

    These textbooks map directly onto courses in a typical EE curriculum.

    • AI Circuits Course (5/5)

      Electronic circuits taught with AI-generated simulations and a learning graph. Built to support the circuits course at the University of Minnesota. 300 concepts, 16 chapters and 75 MicroSims.

    • Signal Processing (5/5)

      An undergraduate signal processing course created with generative AI. 200 concepts, 15 chapters, 34 MicroSims and 150 quiz questions.

    • Control Systems (4/5)

      Upper-division classical control theory covering feedback, Laplace transforms, Bode plots, root locus, Nyquist stability and PID design, told through Gyra the balancing robot. 300 concepts and 16 chapters, but only 6 MicroSims so far.

    • Digital Electronics with AI (3/5)

      An undergraduate course on digital electronics using breadboards, LEDs, buttons and logic gates, with about $50 in parts per student. 300 concepts, 15 chapters and 150 quiz questions.

    • Semiconductor Physics Course (3/5)

      College-level semiconductor physics with interactive simulations, including a PN junction visualization. The learning graph has 600 concepts, but the chapter text is still thin.

    • Networking and Communication (4/5)

      An ABET-aligned textbook on layered network architecture, routing, reliable transport, wireless, network security and programmable networks. 338 concepts and 16 chapters.

    • Digital Design With Programmable Logic (1/5)

      A digital design course focused on programmable logic devices and FPGAs, from a University of Minnesota senior design project.

    • Introduction to Microcontrollers with PIC24 (1/5)

      An introduction to embedded systems using the PIC24 microcontroller platform.

    • MicroSims for Electrical Engineering (1/5)

      A collection of 12 p5.js MicroSims built by University of Minnesota EE students using prompt engineering. Useful as examples of what students can create.

    Graduate and Research

    • AI for EE Research: From Researcher to Orchestrator (4/5)

      A hands-on textbook for graduate students in electrical and computer engineering on using generative AI, agent skills and teams of agents for literature search, data analysis, coding and manuscript preparation. 379 concepts, 20 chapters and 51 MicroSims.

    • A Skeptic's Guide to Quantum Computing (5/5)

      An evidence-based look at the physics constraints, hardware realities and investment risks of quantum computing, for upper-division undergraduates and graduate students. 241 concepts, 17 chapters and 52 MicroSims.

    Embedded Systems, DSP and Hands-On Hardware

    These are good sources for labs, capstone projects and outreach.

    • Real-Time DSP on a $5 Microcontroller (4/5)

      How to benchmark Fast Fourier Transform algorithms objectively on microcontrollers, built around real-time DSP on the Raspberry Pi Pico 2. 574 concepts, 27 chapters and 61 MicroSims.

    • Spectrum Analyzer (1/5)

      Build a spectrum analyzer from a Raspberry Pi Pico and a microphone, with hardware and software tutorials.

    • Learning MicroPython with AI (4/5)

      MicroPython on low-cost microcontrollers through fun projects, with thousands of monthly users. 485 concepts, 23 chapters and 27 MicroSims.

    • Learning STEM with Raspberry Pi Hardware (5/5)

      STEM concepts and computational thinking taught with Raspberry Pi Foundation hardware. 531 concepts, 20 chapters and 99 MicroSims.

    • Clocks and Watches with AI (3/5)

      Design and build digital clocks and watches with breadboards, microcontrollers, real-time clocks and displays, with many complete examples.

    • STEM Robots (5/5)

      Robotics with the Raspberry Pi RP2040 on low-cost kits. 240 concepts, 13 chapters and 26 MicroSims.

    • Introduction to Operating Systems (2/5)

      Process management, memory and file systems, created by a University of Minnesota student as an EE senior design project.

    Introductory Electronics and Outreach

    • Beginning Electronics with AI (1/5)

      Electronics basics for junior high and high school students, taught with MicroSims.

    • Moving Rainbow (4/5)

      Hands-on projects with addressable LED strips and MicroPython.

    • Raspberry Pi Environmental Monitoring: Grades 6–12 (3/5)

      Students build a Raspberry Pi station that measures temperature, pressure and humidity, and learn when sensor data cannot be trusted.

    Math and Physics Foundations

    • Linear Algebra for AI and Machine Learning (5/5)

      College-level linear algebra tied to AI, computer vision and autonomous systems. 300 concepts and 126 MicroSims.

    • Calculus (5/5)

      Interactive AB and BC calculus with 23 chapters and 123 MicroSims.

    • Introduction to Physics (4/5)

      A year-long course covering motion, forces, energy, waves, optics and electricity, with 104 MicroSims.

    Recommended Starting Points

    For an EE instructor, the most complete and directly relevant books are:

    1. AI Circuits Course
    2. Signal Processing
    3. Real-Time DSP on a $5 Microcontroller
    4. Control Systems
    5. AI for EE Research
